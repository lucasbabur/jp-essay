Don't settle

draft.

Ask Claude for an essay and you'll have one in twenty seconds. It sounds smart, says nothing, and you've forgotten it by lunch.

jp-essay makes Claude work the way good writers do. It reads before it writes. It picks a side you could argue with. It writes a rough first draft, hands it to a stranger, and rebuilds every paragraph the stranger didn't remember.

Then an editor goes after the weak sentences and a fact-checker goes after every number, name and quote. You get an essay worth finishing, and every note that went into it.

Sources before sentences.

At least three sources, with facts, scenes, numbers, and quotes. Research records the strongest objection and what the material cannot answer.

Ⅲ

Revise the flawed spans.

An editor identifies what needs work. Each flawed span gets three alternatives or a reason to keep it. Structure comes before sentence polish.

↻

The memory test.

A fresh reader outlines the draft from memory and reports where they got lost or would have stopped. A second reading checks the finished essay.
